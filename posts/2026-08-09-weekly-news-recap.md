# Weekly News Recap: August 3–9, 2026

## U.S. News

**Blanche Confirmed as Attorney General** — The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche, President Trump's former personal lawyer, as attorney general by a 50–49 vote early Saturday, with Republican Sens. Murkowski and Collins voting no.

**Birthright Citizenship Executive Orders** — Trump signed two executive orders expanding the categories of people deemed ineligible for birthright citizenship and targeting "birth tourism," moves the ACLU says cannot override the 14th Amendment.

**July Jobs Report Shocks** — The economy lost 23,000 jobs in July versus an expected gain of about 83,000, with roughly 103,000 in downward revisions to May and June, upending expectations for the Fed's September rate decision.

**Primary Upsets** — Dr. Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, and Rep. Andy Ogles lost his Tennessee GOP primary despite Trump's endorsement, with opposition to AI data centers emerging as a potent campaign issue.

**Fauci Contempt Vote** — The Senate Homeland Security Committee voted to hold Dr. Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress after he invoked the Fifth Amendment more than 100 times in a hearing on COVID-19 origins.

**Michigan Parasite Outbreak** — A cyclosporiasis outbreak has sickened more than 1,500 people in Michigan with the first two deaths reported; separately, the FDA approved the first mRNA-based flu vaccine for seniors.

## World News

**Saudi–Turkey–Pakistan Defense Pact** — The three countries signed a joint defense agreement in Mecca declaring an attack on one an attack on all, amid regional tension over the U.S.–Iran conflict.

**U.S.–Iran Back-Channel Talks** — Tehran says the U.S. and Iran are passing messages through intermediaries over Strait of Hormuz shipping, while Israel's Mossad fired two top officials over a failed plot to topple Iran's government.

**Yemen Escalation** — Houthi missile and drone attacks killed at least 30 Yemeni government troops in Marib and Hadramout, the fiercest military escalation in Yemen since 2022.

**Gaza Ceasefire Strains** — Netanyahu said Israel has not agreed to a U.S.-backed Gaza plan, while UNICEF reports at least 300 Palestinian children have been killed since the October ceasefire began.

*Looking ahead: A total solar eclipse will be visible in parts of the U.S. on Wednesday, August 12.*
