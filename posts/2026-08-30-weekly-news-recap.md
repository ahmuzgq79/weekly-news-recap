# Weekly News Recap: August 24–30, 2026

## U.S.

**Dolly Parton dies at 80** — The country music icon and philanthropist died August 25 in Nashville after a brief battle with cancer, prompting nationwide tributes to her music and her Imagination Library literacy program.

**Supreme Court pauses mail-voting ruling** — The Court paused a lower-court decision that had blocked key parts of President Trump's executive order limiting voting by mail ahead of the midterms, leaving the order's fate for further litigation.

**Deportations to Africa expand** — Internal documents obtained by CBS News show the administration sent more than 100 deportees from Afghanistan, Cuba, and other nations to eight African countries over a 10-day period, including deportees previously granted U.S. protection.

**Judge rules against Pentagon in AI speech case** — A federal judge issued a written order finding the Pentagon acted illegally by punishing an AI company for criticizing the Defense Department's views on AI use.

**Shutdown fight and midterm season heat up** — Congress is battling over a looming government funding deadline while both parties intensify fall campaigns focused on inflation, healthcare, and the federal budget.

## World

**Catastrophic Nepal–China floods** — Flash floods along the Trishuli River, apparently triggered by a glacier collapse near Langtang Lirung on August 26, have killed at least 750 people with roughly 3,000 missing across Nepal and Tibet; the U.S. announced $3.6 million in humanitarian aid.

**Russia pounds Kyiv** — Russian forces launched dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at Kyiv, killing at least 17 people, including civilians at a railway station, amid days of near-constant drone strikes across Ukraine.

**Tanker struck in Strait of Hormuz** — The British military reported an oil tanker was hit by an unidentified projectile on August 27, underscoring continued risk to shipping amid the fallout from the Iran conflict.

**Norway has a new king** — Haakon VIII acceded to the throne on August 28 following the death of his father, King Harald V.

**U.S.–Venezuela oil agreement** — Washington and Caracas reached a bilateral agreement aimed at restoring foreign investment in Venezuela's oil and hydrocarbon sector.

## Tech & AI

**U.S. sets review gates for frontier AI** — The Commerce Department established national security review requirements for AI models that cross certain capability thresholds, meaning top releases now need government review before launch.

**OpenAI's custom chip detailed** — SemiAnalysis published a deep dive on OpenAI's first custom inference chip, "Jalapeno," developed with Broadcom on TSMC's N3P process in just 16 months — a notable step toward reducing reliance on Nvidia.

**AI price war accelerates** — OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 pricing by 80% as ChatGPT reached roughly 1 billion weekly active users, part of a broader wave of rapid releases and steep cost declines across the industry.

**Open-weight models surge** — Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max became the largest open-weight model release to date with a 1M-token context window, while a smaller 27B version brought near-frontier coding performance to single-GPU consumer hardware.

## Economy & Markets

**Warsh leans hawkish at Jackson Hole** — Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation remains "uncomfortably high," pushing rate-hike odds and Treasury yields higher (2-year to 4.35%, 10-year to 4.72%); stocks fell Friday after the speech but finished the week mixed to slightly higher.

**Core PCE still elevated** — The Fed's preferred inflation gauge came in roughly in line with forecasts, but annual core PCE at 3.3% remains well above the 2% target, supporting the Fed's cautious stance.

**Nvidia blowout quarter** — Nvidia reported $96.2 billion in quarterly revenue, up 106% year over year, with EPS of $2.22; shares jumped 8.7% and lifted the Nasdaq (+1.57% to 26,541) and S&P 500 (+0.72% to 7,731) along with chip stocks globally.

**Week ahead** — The August jobs report is the next major data point markets are watching for clarity on the Fed's path.
