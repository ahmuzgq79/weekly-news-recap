# Weekly News Recap: September 7–13, 2026

## U.S.

**Republican convention wraps in Dallas** — President Trump closed the two-day midterm convention on Sept. 10 touting a "Trump dividend," underscoring that he is both the party's biggest asset and its biggest liability in competitive House and Senate races this fall.

**Deadly Amazon cargo crash in Miami** — A Boeing 767 operated by 21 Air for Amazon Air overran a runway at Miami International on Sept. 6, killing five ground workers; the NTSB says one pilot repeatedly warned the aircraft was "too fast," and the crash is raising questions about the safety culture of Amazon's contract carriers.

**9/11 at 25 years** — Ceremonies in New York, Virginia and Pennsylvania marked the 25th anniversary of the attacks, with renewed attention on ground-zero cleanup workers still battling long-term illnesses.

**Shutdown risk eased for now** — With the House having passed (370–48) the Senate's stopgap bill funding the government through Dec. 11, the Sept. 30 deadline looks manageable, a relief after two record-length shutdowns within the past fiscal year.

**Trump in Ireland; Iceland protests** — The president met Irish President Catherine Connolly and Taoiseach Micheál Martin in Dublin during a weekend trip built around a major golf tournament, while Iceland summoned the U.S. ambassador over a Trump social-media map showing the U.S. flag over all of North America, Iceland included.

## World

**U.S.–Iran conflict escalates at sea** — The U.S. military destroyed five Iranian oil tankers on Sept. 8 (after three the prior weekend) in retaliation for attempted attacks on a U.S. warship; Iran responded with roughly 20 ballistic missiles at U.S. forces in Jordan and IRGC claims of strikes on U.S. vessels and Gulf tankers.

**Far-right AfD wins a German state outright** — The AfD took 43.8% in Saxony-Anhalt, more than double the CDU's share and its first-ever state election win, piling pressure on Chancellor Merz ahead of two more state votes later this month.

**Russia–Ukraine war grinds on** — Heavy Russian drone and glide-bomb strikes continued across Ukraine, including a drone hit on a Sumy shopping center that killed two, while North Korea's Kim Jong Un publicly thanked troops in "overseas military operations."

**Sudan crisis deepens** — UN human-rights chief Volker Türk warned of worsening attacks on markets, hospitals and fuel infrastructure and called for an end to the war.

**U.S. Southern Command strikes continue** — The U.S. sank another Ecuadorian vessel in the eastern Pacific, the fifth such incident in two weeks, as the maritime counter-narcotics campaign expands.

## Tech & AI

**Washington weighs global AI-chip licensing** — The administration is reportedly drafting rules that would require U.S. approval for advanced-chip exports to any destination, with light-touch review for small shipments and stricter pre-clearance for large clusters, a potential structural shift for Nvidia and the wider semiconductor supply chain.

**China's Z.AI tops leaderboards without Nvidia** — Z.AI's GLM 5.3 Flash (~320B parameters) reportedly reached the top of global benchmarks while trained and served entirely on Chinese-made chips, a notable data point on how well export controls are holding.

**Nvidia extends the SpaceX partnership** — Nvidia said SpaceXAI will deploy its Vera CPUs for agentic-AI workloads, building on plans for Nvidia-powered AI satellites and orbital data centers targeted for late 2027.

**Steady drumbeat of model releases** — Per industry trackers (details unverified), OpenAI shipped GPT Image 2.5 on Sept. 8, Sakana AI released Fugu Ultra v2.0 on Sept. 11, and Cognition launched its SWE-2 coding model, following Anthropic's Claude Fable/Mythos 5.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra in the first days of the month.

## Economy & Markets

**August CPI runs hot on energy** — Headline CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y, with core up 0.3% m/m (slightly above forecast) even as annual core eased to 2.4%, a five-year low; energy, not underlying demand, is driving the headline.

**Fed hike now the base case** — Futures price an ~80–90% chance of a 25 bp rate increase at the Sept. 15–16 FOMC meeting, a reversal from earlier expectations of no change in 2026, driven by the oil shock and doubts about the Fed's inflation resolve after July's hold.

**Strong August jobs** — Payrolls rose 162,000 versus ~53,000 expected, the best month since March, with unemployment steady at 4.1% and wages up 3.1% y/y.

**Oil above $100** — Brent topped $100 for the first time since July after the tanker strikes and traded near $108 by Friday; analysts warn of $120+ if Strait of Hormuz traffic stays impaired.

**Stocks down on the week despite Friday rebound** — The S&P 500 fell 0.8%, the Dow 1.6% and the Nasdaq 0.7% for the week, though Friday's ~1% gain snapped a four-day losing streak as oil eased. For long-term investors, an energy-driven inflation bump, a possible Fed hike and elevated geopolitical risk argue for staying diversified and avoiding reactive moves.
